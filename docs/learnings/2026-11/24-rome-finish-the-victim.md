## 2026-11-24: Hard Rome took one city and stopped; a human Rome eliminates a small first victim (`rome_opening_finish`, `rome_found_now` on)

- **What a strong human Rome does.** In three recorded games the designer's first victim had one or two cities and was eliminated 0 to 13 turns after the first capture (dead by turn 25, 39 and 33). He made no Peace and razed nothing in 80 turns, opened second wars around turns 46 to 59, and nobody attacked a city he had captured. He reached 6 to 7 cities by turn 50 and 11 to 16 by turn 100.
- **Why elimination matters.** A captured city is frozen (no Production, no purchases) and every unit in it is locked while Rome is at war with its former owner; only Peace or that owner's elimination ends it (`crates/core/src/rules/occupation.rs`). The unit that lands the finishing blow is the one that enters, so the lock cannot be dodged by choosing who walks in. Finishing the victim frees the units and lets the captures build Walls.
- **What Hard Rome did.** After the opening march it took one city, then the ordinary plan chose Peace or Contain on 78% of its war turns after turn 40. In the designer's seats it held 2 to 4 cities at turn 50 and 3 to 5 at turn 100, flat after turn 50. Full-health veterans stood as city guards: about 4,000 idle unit-turns across 200 games.
- **Change.** `rome_opening_finish` 1: the opening picks the softest reachable rival (few known cities, no Walls under way, near; Egypt and Sparta late and at a handicap) and stays on it, with no Peace and no razing, until it is eliminated or turn 50. `rome_found_now` 1: every full-health level-1 melee veteran founds the turn it can on the nearest usable legal site.
- **Evidence.** Rome seat, 200 paired games against five Hard seats: won 3.5% to 7.0% (+3.5 [-0.5, +7.5]), VP share 9.5% to 11.1% (+1.6 [-0.35, +3.55]), cities at turn 100 mean 3.04 to 3.56 (+0.53 [+0.22, +0.84]), a capture by turn 50 in 144 to 154 games, own capital lost 58.0% to 59.5%. Designer's seats (32 salts per map, median cities at turn 100): 5 / 3 / 4 to 6.5 / 3 / 4, against his 11 / 16 / 15. Fresh six-seat games (192 held-out seeds): first victim eliminated by turn 50 in 17 to 53 games. Scripted rusher gate (220 seeds): a Hard city taken by turn 60 37.7% to 39.1% (n.s.). Recorded-attack cells: held-out 29.8 to 30.3, training unchanged.
- **Side effect.** In the rusher-gate games the share of Hard seats eliminated by turn 100 rises from 0.218 to 0.286 [+0.027, +0.109]: Rome now kills a neighbour more often.
- **Measured and rejected the same night.** A Spearmen-only capital (8 melee by turn 50: no more cities, and with the stack on it lost its own capital by turn 100 in 14 of 200 games against 1); lifting the six-city founding cap alone (Hard Rome never reaches six cities from turn 1); pair and group drills (adjacent units already attack); sharing siege hits for promotions (Hard already gets about three veterans per capture; captures are the limit); a recruit making the capturing move (the fighter is the one locked); never-Peace and three wars after turn 40 (captures rise, frozen unwalled captures are retaken as fast); elimination campaigns after turn 40 (small victims are rare by then); waiting out of sight (Hard defenders build Walls on a production timetable, not when they see units: victim second cities are walled 4% at turn 25, 14% at 30, 29% at 35, 44% at 40); assaulting a walled city with Spearmen (a hit does about 11 for 36 HP lost, plus the city's fire).
- **Not solved.** A victim with two or more cities is still rarely eliminated by turn 50: units reach the second city one or two turns apart instead of three together, and a second neighbour sometimes takes Rome's capital while the march is away. After turn 50 Hard Rome starts almost no new wars.
